The read

Where the weight can come off the week

Nothing here asks for a change of role or a new way of working. Each item takes a repeated task off the desk and leaves the judgement where it belongs.

Focus areas

Five places to start

From question 1

The message queue

Most of the day's incoming messages are short and similar. Drafts prepared in advance, ready to approve or adjust, turn the first hour from reacting into deciding.

From questions 2 and 9

Meetings and bulky documents

Site visits, review meetings and long documents all carry the same problem: the decision is buried in volume. One page of notes with the actions and the open questions reads in two minutes instead of forty.

From questions 7 and 8

Searching and typing

Time is lost finding what already exists, and the phone is the slowest place to write. A single place to ask, plus dictation for replies and notes, returns the hours that searching and thumb typing consume.

From question 10

Design approvals

The same review, applied to different projects, from the same kind of information. Fixing the checklist and recording the reasoning makes each approval faster and makes the next one consistent.

From questions 3 and 13

Thinking time

Problem solving and new ideas are the most valuable work in the week. Every hour taken out of admin goes straight back into them, and the five freed hours go to learning.

The line stays where it was drawn

Financials must never leave personal control, so they stay out of every tool from the start. The same applies to anything else named as private. What a tool can see is decided in advance, not discovered later, and the person keeps the final word on every message and every approval that goes out in their name.

First steps

A simple order to do this in

Step one

Start with the reply queue

One repeated task, one week, and a note of what it saved. That is the proof everything else rests on.

Step two

Add meetings and documents

Notes with owners and dates after every review meeting, and long documents reduced to the lines that carry a decision.

Step three

Fix the approval checklist

Write the design review down once, then apply it the same way every time. Consistency saves more than speed.
